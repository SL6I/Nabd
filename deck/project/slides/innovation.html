What is new. Most recovery agents watch service health; Nabd also checks the books, so it sees failures that never raise an alert: every payment needs an order for the same amount, every order a shipment, every queue message must be accounted for. It learns the system’s own laws from four minutes of healthy traffic (129 on our store), so it can catch a failure nobody wrote a rule for. It repairs the damage to data and money, not only the cause, and it proves the books balance before closing. The model only proposes; a fixed catalog, four gates and a person decide anything risky. The value: silent failures cost money before anyone notices, and the learned laws carry over to any system that exposes metrics. Limits, honestly: everything was tested on our own test store; on loud resource faults a simpler method (BARO) ranks the cause better; faults with no known signature need an LLM, and without one Nabd escalates to a person. In a three-fault storm it can also blame a harmless deploy that shipped at the same moment. Next: adapters for Kubernetes, CloudWatch and RDS (the tool schemas are already MCP-shaped), ruling out decoy deploys that the confirmed causes already explain, and a pilot on a real payment flow.
