Mizan (ميزان, “the scale”) catches failures no alert can see. Monitoring asks each service whether it is healthy; in a silent failure every service says yes. Mizan compares independent records instead: every charge in the payment provider’s ledger has an order for the same amount; every order ships and every shipment has an order; the queue’s produced count equals consumed plus waiting, in flight and dead-lettered; the worker keeps moving; our prices match the supplier’s. A service can return 200 and still lose the order, but it cannot make two ledgers agree. When a book is off, Nabd fixes the cause, then repairs the damage, rebuilding promised orders from the ledger, refunding failed charges, replaying lost events, and proves the books balance again. Money above 10,000 SAR always waits for a human.
