Technical approach. Dukkan is the system under test: a small Saudi online store in Docker with seven services, Postgres, Redis, a payment provider, a freight carrier, a supplier catalog and simulated shoppers; faults are injected for real, from bad config deploys to silent bugs. Nabd reads only what an engineer would: Prometheus alerts and metrics, logs compressed by Drain3 into a few templates, Postgres and Redis statistics, and the payment provider’s ledger. It runs the on-call loop: detect, triage, investigate, hypothesize, a skeptic pass, act, verify and report, while Mizan checks the books every three seconds. A router picks the brain: the local core engine for known signatures, incident memory for a fix that worked before, or an LLM (DeepSeek V4 Pro or Claude through an Anthropic-compatible API, with tool use) only for new faults. Every action comes from a catalog of eleven and passes four gates, inside a budget of six actions and fifteen minutes; high risk and money above 10,000 SAR wait for a person.
